# Family Gift Exchange

A phone-first family Christmas gift exchange in English and Spanish, with shared wishlists, encrypted draws, and anonymous questions. Organizers can create a family, add the people who will take part, and give each person a private sign-in key. Members return with Face ID or a four-digit PIN.

## Join and return

Choose **Start a family exchange** with an organizer invitation code from the app owner. Creating the family adds the organizer as a participant. In **Manage my family**, add the other people, households, and any pairs to keep apart before drawing. You can appoint one co-organizer to help manage the family and prepare keys. For someone without their own device, assign a relative as their helper before they join; the relative signs in as themselves and switches to that person's profile with **Help** and their name on **You**. Switching opens their list, or, when their person is ready and still unopened, asks the helper to hand them the phone. A **You're helping** banner stays at the top of every screen; choose **Done** to return to your own account.

From **Send keys**, prepare each person's private message, show a QR code in person, or print a key card. Share privately through your phone's share menu or WhatsApp. Cards include a family address and a six-character code for anyone who prefers typing to scanning. Open the key on the person's own device, confirm it is their phone, and type a four-digit PIN twice on the keypad; dates, years, and easy patterns are refused with the reason. Someone opening a relative's key can choose **No, I'm helping {name}** to send the key to their phone, hand them this phone for their own PIN, or see how to become their helper. Face ID setup is offered immediately afterward; the device may use a fingerprint or screen lock instead. A first key then continues to onboarding: sizes and one thing for their list, both skippable, then **All set**.

After joining, return through the app address, the original message, or a home-screen icon. The app offers Face ID; choose **Use my PIN instead** to return with your PIN. Sign-in greets the only remembered person in that browser, or lets you choose a name on a shared device. On a browser that does not remember the family, paste the family sign-in address or private key link from the organizer. A used key opens PIN entry for its owner. Use **Sign out** on **You** before someone else signs in on a shared device.

Review [Notices](#notifications) for key use, new devices, and replacement keys. Open one you do not recognize and choose **That wasn't me**; **Your phones** lists where you are signed in and offers **Sign out everywhere**, and **Family activity** is visible to everyone signed in to the family. See [Account recovery](#account-recovery) for the effects of reporting access.

## Run an exchange

As an organizer, open **Exchanges** and choose the current exchange, then set its details and **Who's taking part?**. A family can have one active exchange; close it before starting another. Set households, helpers, and keep-apart pairs before drawing.

**Draw names** gives everyone one person to buy for and one person buying for them. At least four people must take part, and everyone needs at least two eligible recipients. The draw excludes yourself, two-person swaps, people in the same household, helper/person pairs, and explicit keep-apart pairs. A fresh draw tries one big loop first and permits separate valid loops when that is impossible; repairs can also produce separate valid loops. Past years do not restrict matches.

Members choose **Open your person** to see their own recipient, then **Open {name}'s wishlist** to open that exact person's list, even when relatives share a name. Helpers switch profiles to open the people they help. Organizers use **Check the sealed draw** to verify the rules, roster, and seal without seeing pairings; they see an aggregate opened count. **Preview as a member** hides recipients and questions. **Test the draw rules** checks feasibility with repeated simulated draws and returns no pairings.

The first opening locks the draw. **Replace the entire draw** then requires the displayed confirmation phrase. A full redraw clears everyone's opened status and all questions, even when someone happens to draw the same person again.

Use **Dropouts and late joiners** to remove or add someone after drawing. Local repairs preserve unaffected matches, opened status, and questions; changed matches must be opened again and lose their old questions. If a local repair cannot satisfy the rules, use a confirmed full redraw with an adjusted roster. After opening their recipient, a member can also use **Something wrong? → Try to fix my match** once per exchange to request the smallest valid change to their match.

After opening your person, use **Ask your person** to send an anonymous question. The recipient answers under **Questions for you**; only the asker sees the answer. Helpers can use these controls for the profiles they manage.

Organizers can open **Practice exchange** to try draws, reveals, questions, repairs, and redraws with a private fictional family. This mode shows all fictional pairings and lets its owner view each participant. It is separate from the real exchange, including any pending recovery there.

Matches remain available after gift day and January 1 until an organizer chooses **Close exchange** and confirms in the sheet; **Not yet** leaves it open. Closing clears pairings and questions, retaining the exchange details and seal, and opens **That's a wrap**. Wishlists remain available for adding gifts throughout the year. There is no later reveal of everyone's pairings.

## Wishlists and gifts

Open **Your list**, then **Add an item** to paste a product link, upload a photo, or write an idea down. There is no per-list item limit. Links work without `https://`, and you can paste the text shared by a store app. **Preview link** fills in available product details; review them, add a note or your own photo, and choose **Save item**. If the store blocks previews or has no usable image, the item can still be saved with the store link and a photo you supply. Use **Edit item**, **Refresh preview**, **Move up**, **Move down**, or **Remove item** to maintain your list.

Choose **Edit sizes and About me** to add sizing, preferences, or allergies. These fields save when you leave each field; wait for **Saved** before navigating away. Select **No clothes, please** if you do not want clothing. The directory's **List ready** label requires at least one item. It also requires tops, bottoms (or waist and inseam), and shoe sizes with a shoe size system, unless **No clothes, please** is selected. To edit a relative's list, [use their helper profile](#join-and-return).

**Family lists** lets you browse every active member's wishlist, even before a draw or for gifts outside the exchange. The directory lists people alphabetically. On that page, an organizer or co-organizer using their own profile can expand **Gift budget** to set one amount in US dollars for the exchange, or leave it blank for no budget. Once the exchange is closed, its budget cannot be changed or cleared, including from a form opened before closing.

On someone else's list, choose **I'm getting this** to reserve a gift. Other members see **Someone is getting this**, without a buyer's name. Claim marks are omitted when you view your own list; while helping someone, they are omitted on both that person's list and your own. Use **Things I'm getting** to track **Bought** and **Wrapped**, or return to the item and choose **Undo mark** to release it. Removing an item clears its mark. Removing a member releases that member's unpurchased reservations so others can claim those gifts; reservations already marked **Bought** remain in place.

## Notifications

Open **Notices** on **You** to read messages; tapping an unread message marks it read. Choose **Notices on this phone** to open **Notifications** and manage device alerts. Organizers can also open **Notifications** from **Manage my family**, where unread messages offer **Mark as read**. The app uses this inbox and optional home-screen notifications; it sends no email or texts. The inbox shows the newest 100 notices, and the unread badge counts only unread notices in that window.

When an item you've marked **I'm getting this** is edited or removed, you receive a general notice without the item or buyer's name. Participating members also receive notices when the budget changes after a draw.

For home-screen setup, open **Put this on your home screen? Show me how** on **You** and follow the instructions for your phone. Open the app from its new icon, sign in, then open **Notifications** as described above, choose **Turn on notifications for this device**, and allow the browser prompt. Enable notifications on each device and after each new sign-in. Use **Turn off notifications for this device** to stop alerts there; the inbox remains available. If permission was denied, allow notifications in your device or browser settings and try again.

Helpers receive device alerts for the people they help on their enabled devices. [Switch to the person's profile](#join-and-return), then open **Notices** to read their messages.

Organizers can open **Manage my family → Messages for the family chat**, choose a topic, and use **Edit** to change its prepared message before copying or sharing through the phone's share menu or WhatsApp. Edits stay with each topic while the page is open. The organizer chooses when and where to send it.

## Run locally

Use Node from `.nvmrc`, pnpm from `package.json`, and Docker Compose.

```sh
nvm use
pnpm install --frozen-lockfile
docker compose up -d --wait postgres
cp .env.example .env.local
```

Set `DATABASE_URL=postgresql://postgres:postgres@127.0.0.1:5432/family_gift_exchange` in `.env.local` for this local database. Generate separate random values for `AUTH_SECRET` and `ORGANIZER_INVITE_CODE` (for example, run `openssl rand -hex 32` separately for each). Keep the auth secret stable across deployments. Give the organizer invitation code only to people the app owner invites to create a family.

For draw development, set `DRAW_DATABASE_URL` to the same disposable local database and generate an `ASSIGNMENT_ENCRYPTION_KEY` with `openssl rand -base64 32`. Keep that key for as long as you need to open the draws it sealed.

```sh
pnpm db:migrate
pnpm db:seed
pnpm dev --experimental-https
```

Set `AUTH_ORIGIN=https://localhost:3000` for this local HTTPS server. Open [the local app](https://localhost:3000) or [captured messages](https://localhost:3000/dev/messages). Seeding adds five fictional practice rosters. Create a new family with the organizer invitation code to practice the full sign-in flow.

Preview the Sweater Weather components, states, and motion in the [design gallery](https://localhost:3000/dev/design). Its controls demonstrate the components without changing family data, including a **Reduce motion** switch. The gallery is development-only and returns 404 on production builds. See the [design rulebook](design/DESIGN.md) for the product direction and [implementation coverage](docs/architecture.md#design-system) for the current screen and component coverage.

To check sharing on a real phone, use a practice family and send keys only to test recipients.

Wishlist photos and preview images use a private Vercel Blob store when `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` is set. Without the token, local development uses temporary photo storage by default. Set `LIST_PHOTO_DIR` to a persistent local directory to retain images across temporary-directory cleanup; a local production server also requires this setting when no Blob token is provided. If photo storage is unavailable, photo uploads fail and link items can still be saved without preview images. Photo access goes through signed-in, family-scoped app routes.

## Deploy on Vercel

Use Vercel Hobby, a Neon Free database, and a private Vercel Blob store. Set `DATABASE_ADMIN_URL` to Neon's direct owner URL (`NEON_DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED` if supplied by the integration), in Vercel Development only. Load it into your local operator shell for migrations and organizer recovery. Use `psql` 16 or later over TLS and supply `FGE_RUNTIME_PASSWORD` for `fge_runtime` and `FGE_DRAW_PASSWORD` for `fge_draw_runtime` through the environment when creating each login. Generate a separate strong random password for each, such as a hex string from 32 random bytes.

```sh
pnpm db:migrate
psql -X "$DATABASE_ADMIN_URL" -f scripts/provision-runtime.sql
```

Rerun the SQL after migrations to reapply the role grants with the existing passwords. Build `DATABASE_URL` with the `fge_runtime` credentials and `DRAW_DATABASE_URL` with the separate `fge_draw_runtime` credentials. Use Neon's pooled host, the database name, and `sslmode=verify-full` for both. Set both variables as Sensitive in Vercel Preview and Production and as encrypted in Development.

- Set `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` from the private Blob store.
- Generate separate random values for `AUTH_SECRET`, `ORGANIZER_INVITE_CODE`, and `CRON_SECRET` (for example, with `openssl rand -hex 32` for each). Generate `ASSIGNMENT_ENCRYPTION_KEY` with `openssl rand -base64 32`. Keep `AUTH_SECRET` and `ASSIGNMENT_ENCRYPTION_KEY` stable across deployments.
- Set `AUTH_ORIGIN` to the canonical HTTPS app address for the environment. Personal links and passkeys use this origin.
- Generate `VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY` and `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` with `pnpm exec web-push generate-vapid-keys` and set `VAPID_SUBJECT` to the public HTTPS app origin. See [notification configuration](docs/architecture.md#notifications).

## Test

```sh
pnpm exec playwright install chromium webkit
pnpm test:all
```

On Linux, add `--with-deps` to the browser install if system libraries are needed. The integration tests use the local Postgres database. Playwright uses the same database, builds the production app, and serves it over HTTPS on port 3100 (`E2E_PORT` overrides this). It also serves the design gallery with `next dev` over HTTP on port 3101 (`E2E_DESIGN_PORT` overrides this); use distinct ports for the two servers. The production test server uses OpenSSL to create a temporary local certificate and gets a generated organizer invitation code.

See [Architecture](docs/architecture.md) for module ownership, database access, and migration conventions.

## Account recovery

For a forgotten PIN, choose your name, tap **Forgot?** on the keypad, and ask the organizer for a new key. The request appears in the family's management screen; any other organizer or co-organizer also receives a [notice](#notifications). An organizer or co-organizer confirms with the person, then prepares and privately shares a replacement from **Send keys → Reset PIN**. Face ID sign-in also offers **Make a new PIN** on **You** for an account that does not require a fresh key. Repeated wrong PIN guesses temporarily lock PIN sign-in; wait for the countdown to end and choose **Try my PIN again**. If PIN sign-in on new devices is blocked, use Face ID, a previously used device after the temporary lock ends, or a replacement key.

Opening or sharing a replacement key leaves reset requests open. Redeeming it closes the requests, replaces the PIN, signs out all other sessions for that name, and revokes its existing passkeys. The person can then enroll Face ID again. An existing PIN remains usable until redemption unless a security report has already cleared it or restricted access.

A co-organizer can prepare a replacement for the organizer during ordinary recovery. An operator can also run `pnpm admin:organizer-key <family-id>` with `DATABASE_ADMIN_URL`, `AUTH_SECRET`, and `AUTH_ORIGIN` set for that deployment. The command prints a private recovery link; give it directly to the organizer.

A report involving current or recorded organizer or co-organizer authority signs out the entire family, including the reporter, and voids outstanding keys. Co-organizer appointments, passkey enrollments, and PINs set or changed from the earliest disputed session onward are invalidated. Credentials directly implicated in the disputed access are also revoked even if older, and the disputed administrators' PINs are cleared even if older. The organizer must recover through the operator command, then issue a new private key for a disputed co-organizer and any other account whose PIN was cleared. Other members can return with preserved PINs or unaffected Face ID; existing restrictions on new-device PIN sign-in still apply. Returning members see a notice that everyone was signed out for safety.

Reports without administrator involvement revoke linked access and unredeemed keys issued from that access, preserving earlier independent keys and credentials. Reporting a used key from its link or code without signing in only records the time and reporting device in account and organizer activity; ask the organizer for a new private key.

**My account** also lists other sessions that opened your person, with device and time. Report an unrecognized opening with **That wasn't me**. The app attempts to repair exposed matches before sealed access resumes. If repair cannot complete, the account remains available and the exchange shows recovery guidance, including a route to the organizer's confirmed redraw. See the [disputed-reveal recovery policy](docs/architecture.md#disputed-reveal-recovery) for the secrecy limits and fallback in small or constrained groups.
